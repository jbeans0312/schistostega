Prepping for Mythic Bastionland[^1] was an absolute joy. The spark table and footnote entries are evocative, comprehensive, and mystically subversive. While prepping my realm's seers and their sanctums, I was served up **The Gut Seer** within a **tranquil cove**. Macabre marine life... how very odd-like.
# SANCTUM (1) - Tranquil Cove
*Warps all sounds into whale noises.*

A beached beast lays on the shore only at low tide. Part whale & squid. Alien smoothness. Cutting it open reveals **The Gut Seer (5,4)**. The beast is swept away by high tide and returns healed the next day.

---
I didn't think it necessary to describe what the cove looked like in my notes, but I imagined it as a teardrop shaped hidden beach, carved from the cliff by the sea. A slippery set of worn steps leads down inside. An important detail I thought of during play was rusty red sand and water to hint at the blood affair to come. Maybe I could have described blood on the walls, but I think that would make the cove feel too hostile. Remember, the sign says **tranquil**.
# Actually cutting it open
My company's encounter with this seer was certainly the session highlight. Just before entering the cove they had met a woman recently stripped of her knighthood by **The Judge**. Since the company was visiting the seer anyway, they agreed to assist in a re-knighting quest should the seer entertain the idea.

I realized upon entering the cove that I hadn't described the beast on the beach, and they had no knowledge of how to actually summon the seer. I did hint at what they might be doing once the beast arrived by describing the sand and water as rusty red, but not how to actually begin the encounter. Thankfully one knight in the company had been knighted by **The Drowned Seer** and had reason to go down and interact with the water. I decided that this would summon the beast, which I described as more whale that squid; I figured that the encounter was sufficiently weird already and didn't need a cephalopodic twist. 

After it leapt from the water and died, the beast's stomach began to wriggle and bulge. The company put it together quickly and cut it open. We play too many of these games. Soon the seer was out in its gutsy glory, and was controlling the whale's eye to investigate the company (a great touch I think). 

The company found some non-verbal ways to communicate with it since their voices were warped into whale song. *I haven't found a great way to facilitate this kind of play in tabletop games. It always boils down to verbally describing how your character moves/gestures etc. when I feel like it could be a very engaging activity.* Maybe it doesn't need to be anything more? This was still great fun, but the opportunity for deeper play is there.

The seer gave them some hints at neighboring myths, and agreed to re-knight the now squire if she was able to find glory with the company. All good stuff. When it was all said and done, the company scrambled up to safety as the sea rushed into the cove to reclaim the seer.

This game is fantastic.
